Week 8 · Nov 5, 2026 · Thursday, 9:00–11:30 am · 280 Brook Street, Room 110

Auditing a federal AI system

Authors: Han Zhang and Codex (OpenAI)

Assess a documented government AI case and explain which judgments the available evidence permits.

Opening

Today

  • Apply the collection and checking skills to a government AI case.
  • Read OMB's 2025 AI use-case inventory alongside the April 2025 M-25-21 requirements.
  • Assess documented practices and identify information still needed to judge a system.

Work and discussion

Use the hands-on task to develop an actual result or question. We will discuss government AI: criteria and alternatives. The eightfold path is a framework we revisit as projects develop.

Class format

A typical session uses 10 minutes for previous observations, 25 for demonstration and guided use of agentic AI tools, 75 for practice including a 10-minute break, 30 for project discussion, and 10 to record next steps. We adjust the balance to the task.

Readings

Assigned readings

  • OMB M-25-21 (April 2025), high-impact AI definition and minimum risk-management practices.
  • Engstrom, Ho, Sharkey & Cuéllar, Government by Algorithm (2020), pp. 6–20.

The federal AI use-case inventory · do it on the right

The government publishes a list of its own AI uses

  • OMB’s 2025 inventory consolidates agency reports of AI use cases into one published inventory (CSV).
  • Record the version and row count of the inventory you use.
  • Use the data dictionary to identify the high-impact designation and the fields describing reported practices.
  • A field records what the agency reported. Supporting documentation may provide more evidence; some judgments will require information beyond the inventory.
  • For the selected system, follow any relevant agency documentation linked from its inventory entry.

One system and its evidence

  • Download the inventory and select one system marked high-impact.
  • Keep the system’s identifier, agency, stated purpose, and high-impact explanation with your notes.
  • Keep the downloaded file and record its source URL and retrieval time, as in Week 4.
  • Use the report to understand agency cases and the published inventory to locate a system. Record what each source allows you to establish.

Scoring a federal system · do it on the right

Course methods that help assess the evidence

  • Use these methods to inspect the evidence for the assigned M-25-21 practices.
MethodEarlier exercise
Compare predictions with a referenceWeek 5, held-out human codes and a confusion matrix
Inspect the data source and collection processWeek 4, source records and the README
Trace claims to their evidenceWeek 6, two claims checked before publication
Compare performance across groupsWeek 7, the subgroup error audit
  • Use the assigned memo’s wording for each scorecard criterion and cite its section.

What a score means

  • For each practice, record one of three verdicts:
    • evidence that the requirement is met,
    • evidence of a gap,
    • insufficient information to judge.
  • Cite the inventory field or supporting document for every verdict. Apply the source checks from Week 6.
  • Where information is insufficient, state what is missing and what would help you assess the practice.
  • Keep each conclusion specific to the system and evidence you examined.

Scorecard discussion

Compare the scorecards in class

  • Two minutes per group.
  • Say what the system does, which agency runs it, and why it is marked high-impact.
  • Give the one practice with the strongest evidence behind it, and the one practice where the public record says least.
  • Name the document or the inventory field you read for each of those two.

What to listen for

  • Whether a documented gap recurs across systems, and where public information is insufficient.
  • Whether a reported practice ever came with a document you could open.
  • Which further evidence would help distinguish a reported practice from its implementation.

Debate groups · do it on the right

Choose a Week 10 governance debate

  • Form a group and choose one of six areas: frontier-model risk; jurisdiction and regimes; geopolitics and chips; power and concentration; the information ecosystem; or labor and the economy.
  • Identify a concrete policy dispute, its actors, a sourced case, and one core reading.
  • In Week 10, present the case for 8–10 minutes and use it to compare policy alternatives in class.
  • Use the Week 10 page to find the shared reading and debate materials.

Project and policy discussion

Government AI: criteria and alternatives

  • What can the published inventory establish about this system? What would require further investigation?
  • What alternatives could the agency consider, and which criteria should govern that choice?
  • Where did the agent help collect or assess the evidence?
